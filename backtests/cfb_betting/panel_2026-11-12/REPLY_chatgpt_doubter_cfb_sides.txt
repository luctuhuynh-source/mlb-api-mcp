THE HOOK: A LEGITIMATE IDEA WITH AN UNVALIDATED CONVERSION

FATAL if the supplied table is used as a universal adjustment.

At an integer sportsbook spread, de-vigging opposing odds estimates the win probability conditional on the bet not pushing. A half-point binary contract has no corresponding push. Those probabilities cannot be interchanged.

Illustration, not an observed game:

Suppose the consensus fair probability of covering -3.5 is 50%. Suppose the relevant probability of winning by exactly three is 11.5%.

At -3, the win probability remains 50%, the push probability becomes 11.5%, and the loss probability becomes 38.5%. The conditional fair probability is 50 divided by 88.5, approximately 56.50%.

Against -110, whose breakeven probability is 52.38%, this appears to create a 4.12-point advantage.

But if the relevant push probability is actually 4%, the conditional fair becomes 52.08%. The same -110 ticket now has negative expected value.

The table can therefore determine whether the engine invents a fire.

Your 314-game figures—11.5% at three, 15.3% at seven, 6.1% at ten and 5.7% tied—leave essential questions unanswered:

1. Are these signed margins or absolute margins?
2. Which team determines the sign?
3. Are they conditioned on the contemporaneous spread being near the number crossed?
4. Which tiers and game states supplied the observations?
5. Were the estimates chosen before examining the apparent opportunities?

An absolute three-point margin can include either team winning by three. Those are different outcomes for a particular team laying three. Even a correctly signed frequency across all games is not automatically the push probability for games priced around three.

The sample size does not cure that mismatch. Even treating all 314 games as homogeneous independent observations, the approximate 95% interval around 11.5% is roughly 8.4% to 15.5%. Conditioning on the relevant market leaves fewer observations.

Also, a 1H table does not validate 2H conversions or pure FCS markets.

What would change my mind: documented signed, market-conditioned counts; audited win/loss/push arithmetic; and validation on games that did not determine the table. The HOOK concept is sound. This packet has not established that its conversion inputs are sound.

MAJOR: At DSB, the voting rule largely collapses.

If a DSB lean can be bet only at fair or better, the PRICE vote is already satisfied. Two of three then becomes:

Favorable price, plus either PERSONNEL or FLOW, with no hard counter.

For a Robinhood or Red105 ticket, the DSB PRICE vote may describe a price the operator is not buying. Its incremental value needs evidence. The actual venue must independently satisfy the price gate.

“Priced at or above fair” is also directionally ambiguous. Higher decimal odds help the bettor; a higher contract cost or implied breakeven probability hurts. That wording is unsafe for an AI-operated protocol.
