# NFL betting - backtests of 2026-11-12: the six proposed engine changes, and a totals track

Asked by Phong, 11/12/2026: "For these 6 rules. Can we use available data and run back tests on to help determine effectiveness before we decide? And should we develop a separate protocol on the Under and Over to run separately from the game so there would be no conflict?"

**Nothing in the rules changed.** Every number below comes from a script in `backtest_2026-11-12/` and its saved output.

## 1. Bottom line

- **The engine changes cannot be tested on DSB.** No history of DSB or Red105 prices exists outside about three weeks of boards. The big books in the odds feed were used as stand-ins instead.
- **At those books, a 3-point price does not exist at the close.** 45 closing snapshots, 532 prices: none was 3.0 points better than the fair from the other books, one was 2.0 or more (it lost), six were 1.0 to 1.9.
- So every fire depends on a venue being slower than the big books. DSB and Red105 are that, and they are paper. The test that matters for real money is Robinhood, and it has not been run (it needs one new tool on the server; the data is free).
- **Second-half leans, against 11 seasons of real second-half lines:** the halftime leader covers 49.9% (1,309-1,312). The FLOW side covers 51.8% (377-351). Break-even at -110 is 52.4%. The lean's structural inputs are coin flips at the market's line.
- **Phong's own 46 half bets, split on price:** 12-5 when the price was at fair or better at the close, 12-15-2 when it was worse (p = 0.12).
- **Totals:** a side and a total in the same half are close to independent (correlation about 0.09). Two Under angles have support on real lines: wind, and a high-scoring first half. Overs lost in every group tested. The flat "1.5 points per half-point" rule is wrong in both directions.
- **A separate totals track is worth building**, on the same price engine, on paper first.

## 4. Stand-in engine test (`13_standin_engine_45_snapshots.py`)

Each feed book takes a turn as "the venue". Its line and price are tested against the fair made from the other books (de-vig each, convert to the venue's number with B-NFL-3, take the median). Edge = fair win probability at that number minus the price's break-even, the same sum the engine does for a direct gap (F-28, H-19).

| Edge of a book's price against the rest | Prices (of 532) | Distinct game-sides | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0 or more | 0 | 0 | - |
| 2.0 to 2.9 | 1 | 1 | 0-1 |
| 1.0 to 1.9 | 6 | 4 | 3-1 |
| 0 to 0.9 | 5 | 5 | 2-3 |
| below 0 | 520 | 90 | 43-46-1 |

- Average price: 2.4 points worse than fair in a first half, 3.6 worse in a second half. That is the books' margin.
- For comparison, the prices in Phong's ledger (DSB, plus one Robinhood ticket) averaged 1.0 worse than the closing fair in first halves and 0.1 better in second halves, and 2 of 46 were 2.0 or more better. DSB hangs softer prices than any book in the feed. That is the whole source of the fires.
- 11/2 CHI@SEA, the one first-half fire (CHI +2.5 at DSB +110, read as +4.0 at board time): at the close BetOnline and BetUS both dealt CHI +2.5 at +115. The price was a market price, not a DSB lag. It closed 0.7 below fair.
- One quote was dropped as bad data (an alternate line at -1000). Nothing else was filtered.

What this cannot say: whether a 3-point price at DSB wins. No feed book ever hung one here.

## 5. The lean layer against real second-half lines (`11_h2_rules_vs_real_lines.py`)

Second half includes overtime. Graded at -110; break-even 52.4%. se = standard error.

| Bet on its real second-half line | Record | Win % | se |
|---|---|---|---|
| Halftime leader (18 of the 21 second-half leans were on the leader) | 1,309-1,312-74 | 49.9 | 1.0 |
| Leader by 1-3 / 4-7 / 8-10 / 11-16 / 17+ | - | 49.1 / 48.7 / 54.2 / 48.3 / 51.1 | 1.8 to 2.5 |
| FLOW side: out-gained the opponent by 1.5+ yards per play and leads (2015-2021) | 377-351-21 | 51.8 | 1.9 |
| Out-gained by 2.5+ and leads | 210-202-14 | 51.0 | 2.5 |
| Second-half favourite | 1,260-1,266-66 | 49.9 | 1.0 |
| Pregame favourite that trails at the half | 471-503-37 | 48.4 | 1.6 |
| Pregame favourite of 3+ that trails by 7+ | 187-218-18 | 46.2 | 2.5 |
| Trailer when the lead is 17+ (the side H-24 allows) | 225-235-5 | 48.9 | 2.3 |
| Market 1.5+ off a line derived from pregame spread and halftime score: bet the side that looks cheap | 312-288-13 | 52.0 | 2.0 |

- Nothing here beats -110. FLOW's 51.8% is just under break-even. At DSB's usual -105 (break-even 51.2%) it would be +0.6 with a standard error of 1.9: not distinguishable from zero.
- The review's FLOW check used a made-up line (half the pregame spread) because no half lines were on hand, and got 50.6%. Against real lines it is 51.8%. Same conclusion, better evidence.
- A derived line is not an edge against the market. That is the idea behind Anchor Mode (H-21), which is already shadow-only. DERIV-LAG is different: it compares a slow book with live prices, not with a formula.

Phong's own bets (`15_ledger_price_split.py`), 46 half bets with a closing price, 24-20-2 overall:

| Price taken, against the closing fair | Bets | Record |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0 or more better | 2 | 2-0 |
| 1.0 to 1.9 better | 8 | 4-4 |
| 0 to 0.9 better | 7 | 6-1 |
| worse than fair | 29 | 12-15-2 |

At fair or better: 12-5. Worse than fair: 12-15-2. Fisher exact p = 0.12. With the coin-flip result above, the working reading is that price carries whatever edge the leans have. That is review decision 2 (bet a lean with live money only at fair or better), still waiting on Phong.

## 7. A separate totals track

**Recommended.** Reasons, each from the rule list or the data above:
- **A total and a side share one slot today.** F-30: one selection per game. H-36: one 2H market per game. The data in 6.4 says that costs fires and buys almost no safety.
- **Totals have no lean layer.** FLOW is barred from totals (F-06, H-38); CLEAR LEAN's three inputs are about which team. When Phong asks about an Over the answer is always "pass".
- **Totals run on side math.** The flat half-point rule and the short key-number list (6.3).
- **Time.** H-32 allows one deep review per halftime, and it goes to the side.

Shape proposed (nothing built):
- **Same run, same price engine, its own track.** One sweep prices sides and totals. The totals track has its own rule IDs, number table, ledger, record and line in RECOMMENDED ("TOTALS: none" when none). No shadow or near-miss is ever printed there (Phong's 10/11 rule).
- **Its own slot:** one side and one total per game per half. Leader with Under counts as one thesis under Liem L5.6.
- **Starting inputs, from 6.1 and 6.2:** wind (10+ outdoors), first-half points (28+), and price against the consensus at the same number using the table in 6.3. **Under-only until an Over angle shows up in data.** The engine can still fire an Over on price, with the wind and 45+ vetoes kept.
- **Paper first.** Liem L5.7 (paper never promotes to full size; micro-live for 30 fills) and L3.2 (60 closing-line entries before promotion).

## 13. Robinhood price history, first pass (added 11/12, about 2 AM)

Change 4, tested with the new server tool `kalshi_get_candlesticks` (free data). Scripts and outputs: `backtest_2026-11-12/robinhood_pilot/`.

**What was done.** For the 45 closing snapshots already on hand (22 first halves, 23 second halves, 10/18 to 11/5/2026), the standing bid and ask of every Robinhood half-spread contract at the snapshot minute: 707 contracts in 45 games. Each side's all-in price (ask + 2 cents; for No, 100 - bid + 2 cents) against the books' fair at that strike (vig removed, moved to the strike with table B-NFL-3, median). Venue rules applied (price 60 cents or less, bid-ask 4 cents or less).

| Strikes within 3.5 points of the books' line | Sides | Games | All-in against fair | At fair or better | 2.0+ better |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First half | 146 | 22 | 2.4 worse on average | 2 | 0 |
| Second half, overtime cannot change the result | 40 | 9 | 2.4 worse | 2 | 0 |
| Second half, overtime could change it (approximate) | 87 | 18 | 1.8 worse | 18 | 8 |

- **Robinhood at the close is as tight as the big books.** The books' own prices in the same snapshots were 2.4 (first half) and 3.6 (second half) worse than fair. The best Robinhood price in a game averaged 0.8 worse than fair in a first half and 0.4 worse in the nine clean second halves. Best single price: 1.0 better. In 31 games with a clean comparison, 4 had any price at fair or better.
- Bid-ask: 1 cent at the median; 99% of contracts at 4 cents or less. The 2-cent fee is most of the cost.
- **Correction, 11/12 3:45 AM (section 17.1):** the split into rows two and three rested on the belief that Robinhood settles a second half on regulation. Its contract text says overtime counts, so every second-half row is like for like. The eight sides at 2.0+ went 6-2, which proves nothing on eight.
- **Far strikes** (more than 3.5 points from the line) look cheap by the table: 112 sides at 1.0 or more better went 26-86 at an average price of 18 cents, a return of about +26%. Table B-NFL-3 was not built for strikes that far out and the 112 sides come from 28 games, several from the same game. Logged as an oddity to test with more games, not a finding.

**Reading.** Scanning the Robinhood ladder at the close would not have produced a fire at the 3.0 bar. A scheduled scan can only pay at moments when Robinhood is slower than the books, such as when halftime opens. That needs the books' fair at those moments, which the paid pull supplies for every 2026 game.
