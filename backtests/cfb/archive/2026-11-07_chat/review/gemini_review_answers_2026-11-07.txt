3. Answers to Q1–Q10
Q1: SuperFLEX
 * Ruling: Make 2 QBs (QB in SuperFLEX) mandatory by default (ADOPT NOW), but REJECT the heuristic preference for a "cheap QB with a rushing role" unless proven in a backtest (BACKTEST FIRST). Allow a 1-QB lineup only if the top available RB/WR out-projects the best available 2nd QB by ≥2.0 FP (matching the C7 swap gate) and is printed for manual override (ADOPT NOW).
 * Reasoning: In the 56-slate backtest, forcing 1 QB lost 3.2 FP (118.3 vs 121.5 FP) and cost 8% in double-up cash rate (62% vs 70%). Forcing 2 QBs scored 121.2 FP (statistically indistinguishable from 121.5 FP) and posted the highest double-up cash rate (72%) and GPP min-cash rate (68%) in the entire table. Furthermore, 83% of the live 10/31 field and 97/100 top finishers rostered 2 QBs. However, preferring a "cheap rushing QB" is a classic single-result story (violating Liem governance) reactive to Castellanos ($5,400, 31.5 FP) on 10/31.
 * Backtest Design (No 10/31 Re-fit): On the 56 historical slates (which end at 2026 W8 and exclude 10/31), test two 2-QB selection methods under v1.2:
   * Pure Projection: Optimizer selects the highest-projected 2-QB lineup.
   * Value/Rushing Tilted: Apply a salary cap or rushing-floor filter to the SF QB slot.
   * Pass Bar: The Value/Rushing tilt must beat Pure Projection (v1.2 with 2 QBs forced, 121.2 FP) by ≥ +1.0 FP with a 90% CI lower bound > 0.0 FP across the 56 slates to be adopted. Otherwise, use Pure Projection 2-QB.
Q2: Props Hybrid
 * Ruling: Revert live builds to v1.2 (100% LineStar) immediately and move v1.3-hybrid to shadow (ADOPT NOW). Run the 6-week historical props backtest before ever putting props live again (BACKTEST FIRST).
 * Critique of Conversion & Rescaling:
   * Missing Scoring Categories: The formula (0.04/pass yd, 4/pass TD, 0.1/rush or rec yd, 0.5/rec, 6 × anytime-TD prob) omits rushing/receiving TDs beyond the first (multi-TD equity)—an massive flaw in CFB where high-usage RBs and mobile QBs frequently score 2+ TDs, whereas "Anytime TD" caps probability at \ge 1 TD (6 \times P(\text{TD} \ge 1) maxes out under 6 FP). Conversely, it fails to subtract interceptions and fumbles lost. This explains why raw props systematically underscored LineStar across all positions, especially RB (0.80×).
   * Selection Bias & In-Sample Rescaling Distortion: Only 151 of 370 players had props on 10/31. Rescaling those 151 players by position (RB / 0.80 = 1.25×, QB / 0.94 = 1.06×) on the target slate itself created two distortions: (a) RBs with props got a +25% multiplier to compensate for the missing multi-TD formula, artificially boosting mid-tier prop RBs (like Turbo Richard and Dierre Hill) past QBs in the SuperFLEX; (b) the 219 players without props were left on raw LineStar, creating an apples-to-oranges player pool.
   * Redundancy: Props already correlated at r = 0.95 with LineStar on 10/31.
 * Backtest Design & Pass Bar: Pull the 6 weeks of historical props (~3,400 credits). Fix the TD math (use Poisson expected TDs \lambda = -\ln(1 - P(\text{Anytime TD})) so \text{E}[\text{TD pts}] = 6\lambda, rather than 6 \times P(\text{Anytime TD})) and compute any positional calibration factors strictly out-of-sample (expanding window prior to the test slate). Pass Bar: Must beat pure LineStar (v1.2) by \ge +1.0\text{ FP} average across the 6 weeks (18 lineups) with a 90% CI lower bound > 0.0\text{ FP} and improve simulated Double-Up cash rate.
