Q4. What three-entry contest split is supported, and what is breakeven?

REJECT — Calling any specific double-up/50-50/GPP split empirically optimal from this packet.

There is no real double-up or 50-50 comparison. The NBA finding cannot establish a CFB advantage, and one break-even GPP slate cannot establish the opposite.

ADOPT NOW — Keep the 1% bankroll ceiling as a risk limit. It is a maximum, not a target or a statement of positive expected return.

The arithmetic depends on actual gross payouts:

- Fixed payout: if a successful entry returns M times its fee, including the original stake, break-even cash rate is 1 / M.
- True 2x double-up: break-even cash rate is 50%. Rake reduces the fraction of entrants paid; it should not be deducted again from a payout already specified as twice the fee.
- Exactly half-paid 50-50: with rake fraction r, no overlay and equal winning payouts, the gross multiplier is 2 × (1 − r). Breakeven is 1 / [2 × (1 − r)].
- GPP: no single break-even cash rate exists without the distribution of payouts.

The actual rake and complete payout schedules are not in packet.

On 10/31, each $30 cash on a $20 entry returned 1.5 times the fee. If every cash were only that amount, the calculated break-even cash rate would be two-thirds—exactly the slate’s result. That is not a general GPP break-even rate. [Packet: Part 5]

Pass bar: require a positive lower 95% confidence bound for the paired improvement in fee-normalized net return before claiming one allocation is better. Require positive absolute net ROI with its own uncertainty assessment before claiming an edge.
